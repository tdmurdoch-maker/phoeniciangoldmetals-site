Our approach

Research, observe, document.

Phase 1 begins with research, basic field understanding, and careful documentation — with stewardship and low-impact practice at every step.

01

Research

Review publicly available information about an area, its context, available records, and relevant rules before field activity is considered.

02

Observe

Make careful field observations within a casual-use / hand-tool posture, with attention to access, permissions, safety, and land status.

03

Document

Keep practical notes and distinguish direct observations from interpretations. Treat findings as unverified until supported by suitable evidence.

Working posture

Small steps, clear records.

Our current posture favors hand tools and a light touch, carried out only with appropriate attention to access, permissions, safety, and applicable rules. We use “exploring,” “researching,” “observing,” and “evaluating” deliberately: those words reflect an early-stage, placer-minded effort rather than a proven deposit or completed claim strategy.

We look for metals, crystals, fossils, and other natural things of value — and we leave the beauty of the land intact. Enjoyment of the land is part of the work; we want a living from nature’s gifts without harming what makes them worth finding.

Any future work will depend on what the evidence supports and on applicable permissions and access requirements.

No promises: We do not promise finds, returns, a discovery, production, or an investment outcome.
